Privacy

Rewi.re runs Sitewire, which makes private, password-protected websites, and lets the people who look after them change and publish them from Claude. This page says what is collected when you sign in to Sitewire, and what it is used for.

Signing in with Google

When you connect Sitewire to Claude, you sign in with your Google account. Sitewire asks Google for your name and email address, and nothing else — not your contacts, files, calendar or mail. Your email address is used for one thing: to check which sites you are allowed to change. The sign-in service itself stores none of this; it keeps nothing between requests.

What is recorded, and where

  • Changes you save. Each change you make to a site is saved as a new version in that site's private repository on GitHub, recorded under your name and email address, so everyone working on the site can see who changed what.
  • Publishing. When you ask for a site to be published, the request names your email address, and the publish and its outcome are recorded with the site.
  • Requests and reports. If you ask for a new site, or report a problem through Sitewire's help, the request or report is kept in a private repository with your name and email address, so it can be answered.

People who visit a site

A visitor to a Sitewire site sees a sign-in page first. Passwords are stored only in a scrambled form that cannot be turned back into the password. Once you sign in, your browser keeps a cookie that lets you in for up to a day. Sites are not listed by search engines.

Who else is involved

Sitewire uses Google to sign you in, GitHub to keep each site's content and history, and Netlify to host the sites and the sign-in service. Nothing is sold, shared for advertising, or used for anything other than running these sites.

Your information

To see, correct or remove what is held about you, or to stop using Sitewire, email sitewire@rewi.re. Removing your access is immediate; the record of changes you made to a site stays with that site unless you ask for it to be removed.
